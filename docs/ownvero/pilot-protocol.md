# Repeated-use pilot protocol

**Evidence state: planned human acceptance protocol; no participant result is claimed.**

## Question and candidate

Can a nontechnical owner preserve an important behavior, check the next AI-built
change and make an informed decision twice, with less confusion than their usual
builder workflow? A passing controlled booking fixture alone cannot answer this.

Before recruitment, freeze source commit, installer SHA-256, runtime inventory,
notices, supported preview contract and site documentation hashes in the candidate
packet. Use a consenting test project and isolated accounts/data. No production
payments, messages or customer-data deletion. Record setup prerequisites and time;
do not hide builder integration or revision-marker work from the result.

## Consent and sessions

The early rehearsal uses 3–5 consenting owners before formal evaluation. The formal
pilot uses 8–10 consenting nontechnical owners. Invitations/contact lists and any
compensation need separate owner authorization. No automatic outreach or telemetry.
Participants choose which observations, timings and sanitized notes may be retained;
recording/screenshots need their own consent. Do not collect passwords or auth state.

Session one: participant describes their real app and an upcoming small change,
confirms 1–3 existing rules, saves a request, reviews a brief, uses their usual AI
builder and checks the candidate. Observe whether they can explain what passed,
what remains unknown and why owner acceptance is a separate choice. Assistance
is allowed for safety and recorded verbatim as assistance rather than unaided use.

Session two: after a real interval, participant brings another real request and
reuses prior rules/memory without the facilitator performing the task. They select
current evidence, spot stale/incomparable results and accept/reject/defer. Do not
substitute a notification click, a second synthetic example or intention to return.

Use the participant's current builder workflow as the comparison. Counterbalance
task order where possible. Record scenario complexity and setup differences rather
than treating unmatched tasks as a causal measurement of time saved.

## Preregistered observations

| Signal | Record | Proposed continuation threshold |
|---|---|---|
| First useful check | Owner explains one named rule and its actual evidence | At least 6 of 10 |
| Repeat use | Second real change completed unaided with prior rules | At least 5 of 10 |
| Better decision | Concrete rejected change, identified gap or avoided rework | At least 3 examples |
| Burden | Setup, maintenance, check time, facilitator help and failure recovery | Compare with existing workflow |
| Trust comprehension | Distinguishes unknown/stale/manual/exception from current pass | Report every confusion and false pass |

These thresholds are hypotheses from the plan, not market benchmarks or a success
guarantee. For fewer than ten participants report numerator, denominator and raw
anonymized observations; do not silently rescale thresholds to manufacture success.
Any false current pass, unauthorized action, credential leak or lost owner data
stops the candidate until repaired and verified. Inability to finish setup is a
product result, not an excluded participant.

## Reporting and release

Use anonymous participant IDs, exact candidate and scenario versions, timestamps,
assistance, outcome and retention consent. Keep raw notes private; publish only
specifically approved anonymized summaries. Respect withdrawal and record deletion.
Report continue/improve/reconsider with contradictory observations and limits.

The final evidence packet must identify a frozen current candidate. G3 is approval
of exact artifacts/site claims. Missing pilot evidence remains NO-GO unless the
owner separately authorizes a clearly labelled unvalidated preview. Approval of
source development does not approve publication or participant recruitment.
